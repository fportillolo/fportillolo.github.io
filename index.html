Undergraduate Researcher

Fourth-year undergraduate in Linguistics at Universidad de Navarra .

My research sits at the intersection of language acquisition, computational linguistics, and neurolinguistics. I use computational models (particularly large language models) as experimental mirrors to study what their alignment with, and divergence from, human behavior reveals about the cognitive architecture of language. A model's mismatch with human prediction is usually more diagnostic of the underlying mechanism than its successes — a child acquires language from a few years of noisy, incomplete input, while a model learns from orders of magnitude more text under entirely different conditions, and where their predictions still part ways is where the interesting questions live. That question, at both the behavioral and neural level, is what I want to keep building computational accounts of over a PhD.

I am interested in how humans acquire, represent, and process language, and in what computational models can reveal about the mechanisms underlying linguistic cognition.

01

Language Acquisition

How children acquire language from limited input, with a focus on predictive processing, cloze probability, and the developmental trajectory of linguistic knowledge in Spanish-speaking children.

↗
02

Computational Models

Using large language models as computational probes of linguistic behavior, examining where their predictions align with human judgments and where systematic divergences emerge.

↗
03

Neural Language Processing

The neural and cognitive architecture of language processing, including EEG, MEG, and eye-tracking methodologies and the relationship between predictive brain responses and computational surprisal.

Francisco Portillo · SSRN Preprint · February 2026

Investigates whether AV-HuBERT replicates the McGurk effect in ways that parallel human audiovisual speech integration. Finds that the model mirrors auditory dominance under conflict but exhibits rigid fusion rates and lacks human-like perceptual stochasticity.
